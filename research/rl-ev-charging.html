Research Motivation

Increasing electric-vehicle charging demand can create new operational challenges for distribution networks when charging decisions coincide with high feeder loading or unfavorable voltage conditions. This research explores reinforcement learning as a way to coordinate charging decisions while explicitly evaluating their impact on the underlying electrical network.

Research Framework

  • Use the IEEE 33-bus radial distribution feeder as the network test system.
  • Represent daily operation with 15-minute time steps and time-varying feeder demand.
  • Model EV charging decisions within a grid-aware reinforcement-learning environment.
  • Evaluate PPO and SAC as continuous-control reinforcement-learning approaches.
  • Compare learned policies with conventional charging strategies and a deterministic model-predictive-control baseline.
  • Validate network behavior using nonlinear AC power-flow calculations in pandapower.

Evaluation Direction

The study is designed to compare charging cost, aggregate charging behavior, feeder loading, voltage performance and network losses across alternative charging-control strategies. The research workflow also separates training, validation and held-out testing so that final RL performance can be evaluated on unseen operating days.

Supervision

This ICREST-oriented research is being conducted under the supervision of Dr. Md. Rifat Hazari, Associate Professor, Department of EEE, American International University-Bangladesh (AIUB).

Current Status

The research is actively in progress for intended submission to the ICREST conference. The simulation environment, grid model, baseline workflows and reinforcement-learning pipeline are being developed and audited iteratively. Final comparative PPO/SAC results are not presented here yet because the study is still undergoing scientific validation.
